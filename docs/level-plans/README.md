# Royal Fiesta — Season 1: Sugarcloud Showdown

Planning only. **18 maps: the five Pass 1 studies retained, plus 13 additions.** No game repository edits, assets, builds or commits. This roster borrows the variety of Fall Guys and the readable obstacle chaos of Stumble Guys from the owner’s `RoyalPartie maps elements.txt`; names and layouts here are original proposals, not copies of those games’ maps.

Season 1 dresses reusable mechanics in sweets, inflatable structures, gummy surfaces, icing, syrup and cloud/balloon decor. Theme is presentation: a generic disappearing floor can become wafer, ice or stone next season; rules must never depend on a candy asset ID. Non-colliding scenery stays outside playable sightlines. Existing candy clouds / balloons are available; brand-new texture/mesh work is proposed only, never built here.

## A match is a five-round show

| Round | Normal entry → qualified | Pool / intent |
|---|---|---|
| R1 / opening | 60 → 40 | B, C1, C2, E, F. Broad starts, forgiving falls. |
| R2 / variety | 40 → 24 | B, C1, C2, D, E, F, G, H, J, K, N, O, P. Prefer a different type from R1. |
| R3 / social or danger | 24 → 12 | A, L, M (12v12); or B, C1, C2, D, F, G, H, I, J, K, N, O, P. At most one team round per show. |
| R4 / semifinal | 12 → 6 | C1, C2, D, G, H, I, J, K, N, O, P. Use the unused mechanic family. |
| Final | 6 → 1 | Q crown grab or I last bean standing; final map reserved before other draws. |

The default has **five played rounds**: `60 → 40 → 24 → 12 → 6 → 1`. A six-round long-show variant adds a genuine cut: `60 → 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 → 6 → 1`, with two opening races before the same 24-player middle, 12-player semifinal and 6-player final. Stage labels in the roster refer to the five-round default. Long-show R1/R2 use the opening pool, R3 the variety pool, R4 the social pool and R5 the semifinal pool.

Normal five-round examples: **E → N → A → J → Q** (60/40/24/12/6/1); **B → P → M → O → I**; **F → H → L → C1 → Q**. The map scheduler chooses compatible types before drawing maps. A normal show targets about 7–11 minutes of play plus introductions and reveals; the six-round variant about 9–14 minutes. These are schedule budgets, not throughput benchmarks.

### Qualification, recovery and exceptions

- **Race:** first quota finishers qualify. At time cap, retain finishers and fill remaining places by ordered stage/lap, then normalised distance along that route to its next gate (not straight-line distance across void), then fewer falls, then disclosed match seed. This is NEW policy: current code only progress-fills if nobody finishes. Finish and CP order must be explicit; current code permits highest-checkpoint skipping.
- **Non-elimination falls:** last valid CP, +3 s wait, spread spawn slots, 1 s non-blocking respawn protection with interactions disabled. No saved CP means own start. Team/hunt/collection use the drawn HOME pads. A fall off a raised objective deck (M/N) also triggers HOME +3 s; ground players legitimately passing underneath are unaffected. B keeps its recovery branch, and the original spiral retains its drawn physical recovery chutes; a chute costs actual travel time, with +3 s only on out-of-bounds respawn. A round can still eliminate a player for failing its score/finish quota; falling itself does not.
- **Team:** A/L/M always split an even lobby equally; winner’s entire team advances, so schedule only when a half cut is intended. 24 → 12 is the normal slot; 60 → 30 is a separate team-special show, not a silent substitution for 60 → 40. No individual team MVP cut. 120 s then 20 s overtime if tied: A next goal, L first net bank lead, M first lead in cumulative zone-seconds. If still tied, lower sum of opening-race ranks wins (all entrants have an opening rank); exact tie uses the disclosed seed (also the fallback in the team-special opener, which has no preceding race). This tournament policy overrides A’s preserved Pass 1 “shared draw” proposal; owner approval is still a design decision.
- **Score rounds N/O/P:** exact top quota. N uses points then earliest last scoring event; O uses hold-time then current possession then earliest latest acquisition; P uses correct matches then total crossing time (unsuccessful cycle = 14 s). Final identical scores use a seed announced before the round. O is cumulative tail-hold, not an all-or-nothing steal on the final frame.
- **Survival H/I/J/K:** no respawn after the stated kill condition. End as soon as alive ≤ quota. A simultaneous fall batch can leave fewer survivors; never revive a loser to fill places. If absolutely everyone remaining falls on the same simulation tick, resolve sub-tick crossing time; an exact tie replays one hazard phase with that tied batch, rather than advancing an empty lobby. At regulation cap with too many alive, up to 30 s telegraphed overtime increases the existing hazard/removal rate. If still above quota, all living beans advance; no invisible score-based survival elimination.
- **Adaptive head counts:** after an under/over-filled survival round, recompute the next nominal cut as `min(n−1, ceil(n × next_nominal / current_nominal))`, with a minimum of 1. Example: 26 survive the 40 → 24 round, so next cut is 13, then 7 finalists; 10 survive 24 → 12, so next cut is 5. Replace a scheduled team round with a solo pool draw whenever n is odd or target ≠ n/2. Final accepts 2–12 normally; larger overshoots add a short E or P cut to 12 before the final. If one remains, crown them immediately.
- **Simultaneous final exits:** use server sub-tick crossing time to determine the last survivor. If exactly tied (or all remaining fall together), replay a fresh bottom hex layer among that tied final batch. This is an explicit photo-finish playoff, not random player-index victory. One-player survivor is champion; no one is revived during ordinary survival quotas.
- **Q crown final:** within 1 m, valid grab input wins. Compare authoritative grab times; exact simultaneous grabs replay from the two equal CP1 approach positions for tied beans. At 90 s, announce 30 s overtime; if nobody grabs by then, move all remaining finalists to CP1 for a fresh crown attempt. Continue until a valid grab; do not award a crown for mere route progress. Final overtime is exceptional and may exceed the normal round window.

### Enough maps for a season

Five opener candidates, thirteen R2 candidates, three team objectives, four survival arenas, three scored non-race modes and two final experiences provide variety without building dozens of near-identical tracks. Reserve I or Q at match start; if I is final, remove it from middle pools. Draw without replacement within a show. Across shows, use separate shuffled stage bags; prefer maps absent from most players’ last two openings (mixed lobbies may have incompatible histories) and alternate final families when possible. Prefer two non-race families and at most two races before the final; do not schedule H then I, or D then G, back-to-back because their skills overlap.

This guarantees no repeated **map inside a normal show** when the required pool has capacity; an exceptional overtime playoff is labelled as such. With only two finals, finals recur every other show; 18 maps cannot promise “never repeat” for a long session. Five opener choices allow five different openings per bag. If a history preference conflicts with a stage pool, keep stage eligibility and no in-show repeats, then relax the across-show preference. Rotate hazard phases, safe tile seeds and starting assignments without counting those variants as extra maps. A third final is the clearest later content expansion, not an unplanned nineteenth season-1 map.

### Elimination show — presentation, not map 19

After results are authoritative, show the entrants in a stable grid (up to 10 × 6 portraits/bean cubbies). Highlight qualifiers, then drop losing cubbies into the decorative void in short batches while the counter decrements, e.g. **60 → 40 REMAIN**. Team rounds mark the losing colour as a group; ties/overtime resolve before this display. Keep it to about 6–8 s, allow reduced-motion fade and skip after the results have been read. It cannot change qualification, spend lives, add physics eliminations or create another course. Finale uses the same reveal to introduce the winner and crown.

## Reading the sheets and jump sizing

The five original sheets retain their Pass 1 N-open labels: in this season N is the stage quota above. C2/D crown-shaped finish pads remain ordinary race finishes, not grab finals. A’s standalone shared-draw label is superseded only in the season playlist by the explicit team tie policy above.

All geometry, numbered zones, legend entries, per-zone descriptions and asset rows come from shared Feature records in `gen-plans.py` and its importable `season.py`. Top views share the original palette, use one metric scale per sheet, a 10 m grid and a scale bar. Dashed route lines are decisions/estimates, not extra walkways; hazard outlines show sweep envelopes. Side profiles exaggerate vertical scale and label it. Every new map has multiple routes or resource/timing choices, more than one play zone and a stated recovery or elimination rule.

**G and I use exploded top plans:** adjacent panels represent vertically stacked floors, not separate floating islands to jump between. G panels subtract 0/90/180 m from drawn x to recover the common world footprint; each full ramp loop rises 12 m. I uses the same x offsets for three aligned tile layers 6 m apart. Their side sections show the true height relationship. All other maps are ordinary top views. P route dashes illustrate one possible puzzle seed, never guaranteed fixed safe paths.

Movement audit: `<repo>/packages/sim/src/character/constants.ts` and `world.ts`: bean diameter **0.8 m**, standing height **1.7 m**, run **8 m/s**, jump **7.5 m/s**, gravity **−20 m/s²**. Same-height jump: **1.406 m apex, 0.75 s airtime, 6 m ideal travel**. Design gaps are normally **3–3.5 m**, plus 0.4 m bean-centre clearance at each edge; use ≥8 m clear landing and a straight run-up. At +0.65 m rise ideal reach is 5.20 m. At +0.18 m it is 5.80 m. No mandatory jump needs dive; existing dive speed is 10.5 m/s, grounded dive lift 3.5 m/s, grab range 1 m.

Missing 6 m H tiles, 5.54 m hex spans and 8 m P tiles are **not intended jump gaps**. Follow adjacent live cells or fall to the next I layer. A 6 m layer drop takes about 0.77 s from rest, so keep adjacent cells below the projected landing rather than relying on a long air-control rescue. K’s low bar top is 0.775 m and high bar underside 2.275 m: standing beans pass under the high bar, but a jump during its pass is unsafe; patterns must provide a clear low-bar jump window. B’s belly speeds and D’s directed launches are new mechanics; C2 uses the existing vertical bounce primitive at a configured 9 m/s.

Choke policies: E has three wide door banks; F keeps a 16 m outside bypass; G has a 10 m gallery plus optional 4 m cut; H/I distribute starts across separate tiles; J uses two 10 m ascents; K has four 8 m transfers; L has centre plus two flanks; M has three contestable objectives; N has 24 caches; O has open juke-wall corners; P guarantees three 8 m edge-connected paths and 16 m goals; Q normally has only six finalists on three approaches. These are geometry decisions, not a claim that crowds have been simulated. Comebacks come from switching away from traffic, preserved CP/score, fresh resources on lower hex layers, stealing an exposed bank, rotating an undefended garden or intercepting a tail loop.

## Mechanics audit and priorities

The local game repository was read only. Assets were checked from every `assets/**/*.asset.json`; usage comes from `courses/*.course.json`. Behaviour checks include `packages/sim/src/course.ts`, `character/constants.ts`, `match/roundSystem.ts`, `packages/game/src/courseSet.ts` and mechanism authoring examples `mech-lab`, `candy-seesaw-run`, `ice-slide-gates`, `neon-piston-alley`, `obstacle-gallery`. Current supported match kinds are race/survival. Player grabbing, surfaces, pendulums, spinners, conveyors, seesaws, moving platforms and basic checkpoints exist. Object carry, scoring modes and crown ownership must not be inferred from those primitives.

**Important current constraints:** courses cap obstacles at 96, collider primitives at 8 per obstacle; convex hulls are not a current collider kind. Hex floors need 381 independent cell states and pooled/instanced collider authoring. P needs 54 independently controlled tiles; H 80 plus support/UI. Long custom decks in Pass 1 also need a primitive-segment representation or an explicit schema extension. `candy.slime-pool` is decor with no collision; `neon.laser-door` is a cuboid mesh, not evidence of a lethal laser sweep. CPs presently respawn immediately to one point. These planning files are not importable course JSON and do not assert that all their proposed collider kinds already exist.
